How to read this

Stage and gauge datum

A gauge reads the water level above its own reference point (its datum), not the depth of the river. Every gauge has a different datum, so levels from different gauges cannot be compared with each other. Compare a gauge only with its own history and its own flood levels.

Data age and provisional data

Levels arrive 15–90 minutes after they are measured, so every reading shows its time and age. Recent values are provisional: ECCC and USGS check them later and can revise them.

“Chance of reaching”

FloodLead’s baseline estimate of the chance that the river reaches a level at any time within the forecast window (for example within 24 hours). It comes from simple statistical baselines, not a hydrological model. It is not an official forecast.

FloodLead baseline vs NOAA official

NOAA/NWS forecasts (Nooksack gauges only) are official and shown unmodified, in blue. FloodLead’s forecasts are experimental baselines, in grey-green; their skill is measured live on the Track record page.

BC gauges have official forecasts too, not shown here yet: the BC River Forecast Centre publishes station forecasts in daily steps (COFFEE, 5 days, during rain events; CLEVER, 10 days) for Sumas River near Huntingdon and Chilliwack River at Vedder Crossing, plus basin flood advisories. What each official product gives, and how it did in 2021 and 2025: what Sumas Prairie farmers have today (a sourced review).

What the ledger proves

Every FloodLead forecast is fixed in a public, hash-chained ledger before the outcome is known, and the chain is published hourly to GitHub. Changing any past forecast would break the chain, so the track record cannot be edited afterwards. Anyone can verify it: see the Track record page.
