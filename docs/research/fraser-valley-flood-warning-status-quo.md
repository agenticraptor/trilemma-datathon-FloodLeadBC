# Closing the Everson-to-barn warning gap

Sumas Prairie farmers no longer lack upstream warning. In December 2025 the US National Weather Service (NWS) flagged the Nooksack threat five days out and named Abbotsford roughly 29–37 hours before water crossed the border, and Abbotsford's evacuation alert came about 12–18 hours ahead of the water. That is a real advance on November 2021, when orders followed the water and hundreds of thousands of farm animals died. What is still missing is the last mile from the Everson overflow to the barn: no official product gives a calibrated probability, forecasts when and how much overflow water will reach a given farm, pushes warnings to every farmer's phone at night across the Abbotsford–FVRD–Chilliwack split, or publishes its own accuracy. Both floods exposed the same weak spots: a US crest forecast about 2 ft too low a day ahead, a "major" upgrade issued only after overflow had begun, a 7-hour travel rule that 2025's slower arrival broke, and evacuation orders issued at night. FloodLead BC can be measurably better on exactly those points (hourly timing, calibrated exceedance probabilities, arrival estimates keyed to the Everson overflow gauges, staged personal action levels, direct opt-in SMS and a public scored ledger), but not on 24–48-hour rain-driven magnitude, the River Forecast Centre's Fraser freshet forecasts, inundation depth, dike breaches, or the legal and reimbursement role of official alerts. In replays of both floods, plain automation (relaying existing warnings and watching the overflow gauges) would have reached farmers about 5–7 daylight hours before Abbotsford's 2025 alert and about 9 hours before its first 2021 alert; any gain from genuine forecast skill beyond that is unproven. The "thank God" bar is a trusted daylight call at least 24 hours before water reaches the farm, arrival times within about 3 hours, and false alarms tiered to the cost of each action, demonstrated against archived NWS, River Forecast Centre and City decisions. That proof will take years, because big overflows are rare and the planned 2026 replacement of the SR 544 overflow culvert with a bridge changes the hydraulics that matter most.

## A dozen warning channels, none forecasting water at the farm gate

Today's warning chain has three layers, and each one trades lead time for precision. County- and region-scale weather warnings come one to five days out: NWS Seattle issued a Flood Watch covering Whatcom County at **4:10 PM on Dec 5, 2025, about five days before the Nooksack overflowed** ([IEM NWS archive](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FFASEW&sdate=2025-11-28&edate=2025-12-22&fmt=text&limit=600)), and Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC), which switched to yellow/orange/red impact-coded warnings on Nov 26, 2025, put the central Fraser Valley under an orange rainfall warning and Abbotsford under a yellow one on Dec 9 ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11543316/canada-new-alert-system-extreme-weather/); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11570446/atmospheric-river-bc-south-coast-tuesday-2/)). River warnings from the BC River Forecast Centre (RFC) and NWS come from hours to about a day out. Property-level evacuation alerts and orders from Abbotsford, the Fraser Valley Regional District (FVRD) and Chilliwack come hours out. Real-time gauges on both sides of the border confirm what is happening but forecast nothing.

The RFC's public flood products are text advisories in three tiers (High Streamflow Advisory, Flood Watch, Flood Warning), defined relative to bankfull, issued for whole basins such as "Fraser Valley East", and posted as PDFs with no push or SMS subscription ([BC RFC warnings](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/warnings/index.htm)). The RFC also publishes station-level numbers: the 5-day COFFEE model and the 10-day CLEVER model both forecast **Sumas River near Huntingdon (08MH029)** and **Chilliwack River at Vedder Crossing (08MH001)** ([COFFEE table](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/fallfloods/coffee/S-COAST.PDF); [CLEVER table](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/freshet/clever/CLM_FOR_FRASER_LW_10DAYS.PDF)). These numbers are coarse. They come in daily steps and are deterministic; their "upper" and "lower" bounds are built from past error statistics, and the model's own technical reference says its "ensemble" is not a set of forecasts ([COFFEE technical reference](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/fallfloods/TechnicalRefCOFFEEModel.pdf)). COFFEE runs only when a coastal rain event is under way or forecast, applies no bias correction to rainfall, and refreshes its "observed" values once a day from a midnight-to-8 AM reading; CLEVER updates once or twice a week outside the freshet ([COFFEE page](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/fallfloods/map_coffee.html); [COFFEE guide](http://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/fallfloods/coffee_for_chart.pdf); [CLEVER page](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/freshet/map_clever.html)). The CLEVER guide itself warns that forecasting atmospheric rivers "is extremely difficult and challenging" ([CLEVER guide](http://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/freshet/for_chart.pdf)). Nothing the RFC publishes forecasts the Nooksack overflow: its 10 AM Dec 10, 2025 Flood Warning relayed the US forecast of a **148.48 ft crest at North Cedarville** and called 2021-like levels "a lower-likelihood, but possible scenario" ([RFC Flood Warning, Dec 10, 2025](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/warnings/advisories/FWN_2025_12_10_1000_SC_upg.pdf)). Its most forward-looking product, the 7-Day Streamflow Hazard Outlook, is emailed to officials and "is not intended for the general public" ([RFC outlook](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/outlooks/BC_7-Day_Streamflow_Hazard_Outlook.pdf)). In 2021 the RFC had **5.5 full-time staff, against 16 at the US Northwest River Forecast Center** ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6270315)); no current figure was found.

The US side supplies most of the useful upstream lead time. NWS and the Northwest River Forecast Center (NWRFC) issue routine, year-round deterministic forecasts for the **Nooksack at North Cedarville (NRKW1)**, running about seven days ahead in 6-hour steps, against thresholds of 144.8 ft (action), 146.5 ft (minor), 148 ft (moderate) and 150 ft (major). At major stage the official impact statement warns that water "could overflow and flood the Sumas drainage" ([NWPS NRKW1](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/NRKW1); [NWPS forecast](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/NRKW1/stageflow)). NWS also issues an areal Flood Warning for the Everson overflow corridor, whose polygon runs to the border. It did so in 2021 as well, and after Transboundary Flood Initiative work in 2024 it added new impact statements and bulletins for the corridor ([BC TFI page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/drought-flooding-dikes-dams/integrated-flood-hazard-management/governance/nooksack-sumas-tfi); [IEM, 2021 warnings](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FLWSEW&sdate=2021-11-10&edate=2021-11-20&fmt=text&limit=400)). The overflow channel is watched by USGS gauges at SR 544 (NOEW1) and Emerson Road (NEEW1) plus webcams, but they are observation-only ("Forecasts are not available"), and the Emerson Road gauge, whose record only began in January 2024, was **out of service on Oct 9, 2026** ([NWPS NOEW1](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/NOEW1); [NWPS NEEW1](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/NEEW1); [USGS series catalog](https://waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/site/?format=rdb&sites=12211195,12211190,12211200,12210700,12214500&seriesCatalogOutput=true&outputDataTypeCd=iv)). NWS does not send wireless emergency alerts for river or areal flood warnings, so US phone alerts depend on Whatcom County's own systems and the City of Sumas siren, which serve US residents ([NWS WEA](https://www.weather.gov/wrn/wea); [Cascadia Daily News](https://www.cascadiadaily.com/news/2022/oct/06/county-officials-and-emergency-responders-planning-future-flood-procedures/)).

On the Canadian side, delivery is fragmented and mostly opt-in. Abbotsford's alert sign-up is email-only. In December 2025 the City used its website, social media, EmergencyInfoBC, an address-search evacuation map and hand-delivered notices, and none of the City emergency pages reviewed for this report mention Alertable or sirens ([Abbotsford Alerts](https://www.abbotsford.ca/alerts); [Abbotsford bulletin](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/evacuation-orders-issued-parts-sumas-prairie-west-and-evacuation-alert-issued)). The FVRD's electoral areas and the City of Chilliwack use the commercial Alertable app with SMS and phone calls ([FVRD](https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/emergency-management/emergency-notifications.html); [Chilliwack](https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=907)), while the Township of Langley relies on its website and eNews ([Township of Langley](https://www.tol.ca/en/services/flooding.aspx)). No single subscription covers Sumas Prairie, which straddles Abbotsford and the Chilliwack side. The only channel that reaches every phone without sign-up, BC Emergency Alerts (Alert Ready), has been open to local governments for floods since May 2022, but it is reserved for imminent threats to life and has **never been used for a Fraser Valley flood**; the 2021–2025 archive shows flood alerts only for the 2024 Chilcotin landslide ([BC alert archive](https://catalogue.data.gov.bc.ca/dataset/bc-emergency-alert-archive); [How alerts work](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/public-safety/emergency-alerts/how-alerts-work); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8807687/former-okanagan-evacuees-bc-expansion-alert-ready-system/)). The province's flood page for producers tells them to watch the RFC and ECCC and says nothing about local alerts or US sources ([BC producer flood page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/agriculture-seafood/business-market-development/emergency-management/freshet-and-flood)).

Three things are absent from every channel. None gives a calibrated probability; BC's bounds are historical error envelopes and the US forecasts are single numbers. None forecasts how much overflow water will cross the border, when it will arrive, or how deep it will be at a given farm. Abbotsford bridges that gap with non-public provincial hydraulic modelling, 1990, 2020 and 2021 analogues, and the rule of thumb that "historically, it has taken approximately 7 hours for the water to reach Abbotsford" ([Abbotsford bulletin, Dec 10, 2025](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/update-heavy-rain-and-flood-warning)). And none publishes a track record: no verification statistics were found for the RFC's Fraser Valley products or for NWRFC's Nooksack forecasts; the only public NWRFC verification document is a single-event case study from 2006 ([BC RFC warnings](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/warnings/index.htm); [NWRFC case study](https://www.weather.gov/media/owp/oh/rfcdev/docs/NWRFC_national_verification_case_study_RFCworkshop_Nov08_Part1.pdf)). Google Flood Hub lists Canada and the US among covered countries, but no Flood Hub gauge on the Nooksack, Sumas or Chilliwack could be confirmed, and no farmer or agency here was found using it ([Flood Hub help](https://support.google.com/flood-hub/answer/16508958?hl=en)).

## 2021 orders trailed the water; 2025 alerts led it by half a day

In November 2021 the forecast was not the problem. NWS issued a hydrologic outlook on Nov 8 and a Flood Watch for Whatcom County on Nov 10, and at **3:40 PM on Sunday, Nov 14 it warned in plain text that the Nooksack would overflow at Everson and flood "toward Sumas and the Canadian border"** ([IEM, 2021 warnings](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FLWSEW&sdate=2021-11-10&edate=2021-11-20&fmt=text&limit=400); [IEM, 2021 watches](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FFASEW&sdate=2021-11-06&edate=2021-11-20&fmt=text&limit=600)). The SR 544 overflow gauge began rising about 6:25 PM ([USGS, Nov 2021](https://nwis.waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/iv/?format=rdb&sites=12211200,12211190,12211195,12215100,12214550,12215000,12214500&parameterCd=00065,00060&startDT=2021-11-13T00:00-08:00&endDT=2021-11-19T00:00-08:00)). Abbotsford's first alerts touching Sumas Prairie came shortly after midnight, about 9 hours after the US warning. By 1:20 PM Monday farmers were already moving livestock out of swamped barns; orders for parts of the prairie came that evening, police and search-and-rescue were using boats at Whatcom and North Parallel roads by about 10:20 PM, and the order for the whole prairie came at about 6:45 AM on Nov 16 ([Vancouver Sun live blog](https://vancouversun.com/news/local-news/significant-atmospheric-river-causing-rainfall-warnings-across-southern-b-c); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/coquihalla-trans-canada-highway-damage-1.6250235)). The City declined to use Alert Ready ("We didn't want to alarm the whole city") and relied on door-knocking ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8380118/b-c-flooding-abbotsford-rescue-by-water-air-heavy-flooding/amp)). Dairy farmer Chelsea Meier woke to water at about 4 AM on Nov 16: "We got no warning" ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/meier-farm-sumas-prairie-recover-floods-abbotsford-warning-system-failure-1.6268183)). More than 180 people were rescued overnight and hundreds of farmers refused to leave their animals ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bc-agriculture-animals-flood-1.6252774)). The province counted **628,000 poultry, 420 dairy cattle and about 12,000 hogs dead** (BC Dairy put cattle deaths near 500), and province-wide the floods hit more than 1,000 farms with about $285 million in farm damage ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8421653/bc-floods-animals-killed-fraser-valley/amp); [BC Dairy](https://bcdairy.ca/press-releases/bc-dairy-update-cattle-evacuation-milk-supply/); [Senate report](https://sencanada.ca/content/sen/committee/441/AGFO/reports/2022-10-27_B.C.Floods_AltText_e.pdf)). On the night of Nov 16 the Barrowtown pump station, which "was never intended or designed to take on water from another country", nearly failed ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8382138/abbotsford-mayor-hopeful-some-flood-waters-recede/amp/)).

December 2025 ran differently. The RFC's Flood Watch for Fraser Valley East named "the potential for spillover from the Nooksack River" by **12:19 PM Dec 9** ([EmergencyInfoBC](https://www.emergencyinfobc.gov.bc.ca/event/floodwatch-fraservalley-09122025/)), and NWS's first North Cedarville warning at **10:17 PM Dec 9** said the river "could lead to an Everson overflow flood event that would also impact Nooksack, Sumas, into Abbotsford BC Canada" ([IEM, 2025 warnings](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FLWSEW&sdate=2025-11-28&edate=2025-12-22&fmt=text&limit=600)). The RFC upgraded to a Flood Warning at 10 AM Dec 10, NWS declared overflow "imminent or occurring" at 10:26 AM, and the Emerson Road overflow gauge began rising at 2:30 PM, with SR 544 following at about 5:15 PM ([USGS, Dec 2025](https://nwis.waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/iv/?format=rdb&sites=12211200,12211190,12211195,12215100,12214550,12215000,12214500&parameterCd=00065,00060&startDT=2025-12-08T00:00-08:00&endDT=2025-12-14T00:00-08:00)). Abbotsford issued evacuation alerts for Sumas Prairie West and Clayburn Village between about 4 and 5:20 PM, with hand-delivered notices to the most exposed properties, then declared a local emergency and ordered 371 properties out at about 11 PM ([Abbotsford News](https://abbynews.com/2025/12/10/evacuation-order-issued-for-clayburn-village-sumas-prairie-west/); [EmergencyInfoBC](https://www.emergencyinfobc.gov.bc.ca/event/flood-alert-abbotsford-10dec25/); [Fraser Valley Current](https://fvcurrent.com/p/flood-warning-issued-as-atmospheric-river-drenches-eastern-fraser-valley)). The City reported water crossing into Abbotsford in its Dec 11 morning bulletin, and the Vye Road dike breached that afternoon ([Abbotsford bulletin](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/floodwaters-cross-abbotsford-and-evacuation-orders-expanded); [Country Life in BC](https://www.countrylifeinbc.com/sumas-flooding-spurs-call-for-action/)). That gave roughly **12–18 hours from alert to water at the border and about 22 hours to the dike breach** (reconstructed estimates). Farmers noticed the difference. "This time we heard the sirens, but we still had lots of time," a farm-market owner said (probably the Sumas, Washington siren; no Abbotsford siren is on record), the agriculture minister said farmers "feel more prepared", and BC Dairy called the City's emergency communication "clear and consistent" ([The Cascade](https://ufvcascade.ca/the-2025-floods-effect-on-abbotsfords-farmers/); [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-fraser-valley-flooding/); [BC Dairy](https://bcdairy.ca/press-releases/statement-from-bc-dairy-on-flood-conditions-in-the-fraser-valley/)).

The forecasts made the same error twice. NWS's first North Cedarville warnings called for "moderate" flooding with crests of **148.9 ft in 2021 and 148.4 ft in 2025**; the river reached **150.76 ft and 150.49 ft**. That is about 2 ft too low at roughly a day's lead, which is exactly the gap between "moderate" and "major". In both events the upgrade to "major", the category whose impact statement mentions the Sumas drainage, came only after overflow had begun and about 10 hours before the crest. The Canadian Flood Warning inherited the low number. This matches the published record elsewhere. Verification of NWS stage forecasts on central US rivers found forecasts above flood stage biased low, with the bias growing with lead time and skill over persistence (simply assuming the river stays where it is) fading to about zero by day 3 ([Welles 2005](https://www.weather.gov/media/owp/oh/rfcdev/docs/EdwinWellesV6.pdf)). The one public NWRFC case study found forecasts "generally low", with forecast rainfall the biggest error source ([NWRFC case study](https://www.weather.gov/media/owp/oh/rfcdev/docs/NWRFC_national_verification_case_study_RFCworkshop_Nov08_Part1.pdf)). Even the reference crest is contested: the NWS database ranks 2025 just below 2021, Whatcom County called 2025 a record, and the NWRFC station page still lists a 2006 record of 149.61 ft ([Whatcom County](https://www.whatcomcounty.us/m/newsflash/home/detail/5274); [NWRFC](https://www.nwrfc.noaa.gov/river/station/flowplot/flowplot.cgi?lid=NRKW1)).

North Cedarville also proved a poor proxy for what reaches Canada. Its 2025 crest came within about 0.3 ft of 2021's, yet the SR 544 overflow peaked at **6.91 ft against 7.75 ft** and lasted roughly 27 hours against 44, and Abbotsford rated the 2025 event about 1-in-35 years against 2021's 1-in-100 ([NWPS NOEW1](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/NOEW1); [Abbotsford flood response](https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse)). The provincial and City magnitude call was right: flooding "more than what was seen in 2020 but not as significant as the 2021 event", later "tracking similarly to the 1990 flood" ([Abbotsford alerts bulletin](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/evacuation-alerts-issued-sumas-prairie-west-and-clayburn-village); [Abbotsford bulletin, Dec 11](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/floodwaters-cross-abbotsford-and-evacuation-orders-expanded)). Whatcom officials had named the core problem in 2022: "We might be confident we're going to have overflow, we just don't know how much" ([Cascadia Daily News](https://www.cascadiadaily.com/news/2022/oct/06/county-officials-and-emergency-responders-planning-future-flood-procedures/)). Sediment building up near Everson, a culvert damaged in 2021 and levees damaged in 2025 keep changing how much water spills north ([USGS Nooksack geomorphology](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/washington-water-science-center/science/nooksack-geomorphology); [WA Ecology TFI](https://ecology.wa.gov/ecologys-work-near-you/river-basins-groundwater/nooksack-transboundary-flood-initiative)).

Timing was the weakest link in 2025. Counted from the gauge-observed onset, the City's 7-hour rule implied water at the border between about 9:30 PM and midnight on Dec 10. The 11 PM order projected arrival "in the early hours" of Dec 11, the City reported water crossing only in its morning bulletin, and a farm-market owner said afterwards that "it [now] takes about 20 hours" ([Abbotsford bulletin](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/update-heavy-rain-and-flood-warning); [Fraser Valley Current](https://fvcurrent.com/p/flood-warning-issued-as-atmospheric-river-drenches-eastern-fraser-valley); [The Cascade](https://ufvcascade.ca/the-2025-floods-effect-on-abbotsfords-farmers/)). No source records the actual crossing time, so the realized travel time can only be bounded, at roughly 10–20 hours, well beyond the rule either way. When the water came, it came fast: "Everything was fine until about an hour ago and then all of a sudden it just went crazy" ([CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/all-of-a-sudden-it-just-went-crazy-flooding-on-sumas-prairie-more-evacuation-orders/)). Orders went out at 11 PM, so door-knocking and livestock moves ran through the night ([Abbotsford bulletin, Dec 11](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/floodwaters-cross-abbotsford-and-evacuation-orders-expanded)). Highway 1 closed reactively at about 8:16 PM on Dec 11 once water was on the road ([Fraser Valley Current](https://fvcurrent.com/p/flood-warning-issued-as-atmospheric-river-drenches-eastern-fraser-valley)), and a motorist died in a flooded ditch on Huntingdon Road on Dec 13 ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11576710/bc-communities-high-risk-flooding-atmospheric-river)). A recirculated 2021 evacuation order caused "undue panic" ([Abbotsford bulletin, Dec 11](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/floodwaters-cross-abbotsford-and-evacuation-orders-expanded)). Alert Ready again went unused; the province said only that it was prepared to send "broadcast-intrusive" alerts ([CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/a-very-dynamic-situation-bc-officials-give-update-on-flood-risk-as-evacuations-ordered/)).

Two cautions apply. The 2025 flood was smaller, so its better outcome ("some poultry" lost and a few barns flooded, against hundreds of thousands of animals in 2021) cannot be credited to warnings alone ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11575062/bc-fraser-valley-flooding); [Country Life in BC](https://www.countrylifeinbc.com/sumas-flooding-spurs-call-for-action/)). And no official after-action review of warning timeliness was found for either event. The province says "improved cross-border flood forecasting and warning systems" produced "earlier alerts" in 2025, but gives no metrics ([BC TFI page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/drought-flooding-dikes-dams/integrated-flood-hazard-management/governance/nooksack-sumas-tfi)). The clearest warning recommendation came from an independent community report: "The early warning systems between municipalities, provinces, and Washington State should be strengthened" ([Archway, 2023](https://archway.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/After-the-Flood-Report-Final.pdf)). In 2021 that link was a morning phone call: ahead of the second overflow on Nov 28, the mayor promised to "watch that Nooksack like a hawk" ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/abbotsford-mayor-nooksack-river-heavy-rains-forecast-nov-27-1.6265548)). What changed by 2025 was mostly institutional: shared Whatcom and provincial modelling, NWS overflow bulletins revised with Canadian partners, pre-emptive alerts and hand delivery. The public forecasts did not change; they were still deterministic, still low a day out, and still silent on arrival time.

## Farmers move in stages, and each stage needs daylight

Farmers on the prairie learn about floods mostly from people. In 2021 Meier was woken by a floating steel drum, a neighbour towed hay farmer Cindy Dueck out by tractor, and Yarrow dairy farmer Grant Bouwman heard about the dike breach while helping move his father-in-law's cattle ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/meier-farm-sumas-prairie-recover-floods-abbotsford-warning-system-failure-1.6268183); [Western Producer](https://www.producer.com/news/were-going-to-come-back-from-it/)). The premises ID registry, which Meier understood would warn farms, did not ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/meier-farm-sumas-prairie-recover-floods-abbotsford-warning-system-failure-1.6268183)). The poultry sector's emergency lead had to gather information from Environment Canada because the industry could not get Abbotsford's situation reports ([Country Life in BC](https://www.countrylifeinbc.com/farmers-step-up-in-emergency-operations/)). In a 2022 FVRD survey of neighbouring rural areas, nearly a third of affected residents first learned of alerts or orders through social media and only 20% through Alertable ([FVRD survey](https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/2022-03-25%20FVRD-Extreme-weather-response-survey.pdf)). In 2025 the trusted channels were the City's alerts and door-knocks and the industry associations sitting in the City's emergency operations centre ([BC Dairy](https://bcdairy.ca/press-releases/statement-from-bc-dairy-on-flood-conditions-in-the-fraser-valley/)). No source shows farmers reading NWS Nooksack hydrographs themselves, even though Abbotsford linked to them in its bulletins ([Abbotsford bulletin](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/update-heavy-rain-and-flood-warning)).

Before a flood, provincial guidance asks for actions on very different clocks. Farmers should empty manure pits and spread the manure "two weeks prior to expected flooding", move non-milking stock "in the days leading up to potential flooding", move bulls early, line up temporary milking and a list of haulers "who can assist on short notice", ship near-market birds and hogs to processors, move poultry upstairs in two-storey barns, and tell the milk hauler and vet where animals have gone ([BC agriculture factsheet](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/farm-management/emergency-management/emergency_preparedness_tips_for_flooding_livestock_sector.pdf)). Abbotsford now offers an approval route for berms that protect homes and barns "from a Nooksack River overflow event" ([Abbotsford berms FAQ](https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse/berms-faq)). BC Dairy runs buddy-farm relocation and an emergency workbook, and the BC Poultry Association keeps a standing industry operations centre that in 2021 activated "before some of the government and ministries did" ([Country Life in BC](https://www.countrylifeinbc.com/producers-urged-to-make-emergency-plans/); [Country Life in BC](https://www.countrylifeinbc.com/farmers-step-up-in-emergency-operations/)). None of these plans is tied to a forecast trigger or a stated lead time.

During a flood, farmers act in stages: cheap, reversible moves first, expensive ones only on confirmation. With "a couple of days" of awareness in 2025, Matt Dykshoorn moved young cows out of a low barn that later took five feet of water and kept his milking herd home; across the prairie "a few dairies moved milking herds to higher ground and evacuated heifers and non-milking cows", some poultry and hogs left, and most livestock stayed ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11576273/abbotsford-mayor-blasts-feds-inaction-flood-response-farmers-cleanup); [Country Life in BC](https://www.countrylifeinbc.com/sumas-flooding-spurs-call-for-action/)). Even experienced farmers hesitate without visible water. In 2021 Amanda Bouwman told her husband, "Grant, there's green grass, there's no water, the dikes are fine", and it took friends and family to coax them into moving ([Western Producer](https://www.producer.com/news/were-going-to-come-back-from-it/)). Without lead time, rescue replaces evacuation. In 2021 cattle were moved by boat, jet ski and canoe, and many farmers "attempted to move animals and then had to walk away" ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8393291/farmers-volunteers-save-livestock-floods-bc); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bc-agriculture-animals-flood-1.6252774)).

The cases imply a rough lead-time ladder; this is an inference from documented cases, not a published standard. **Under two hours, only rescue is possible.** **Six to ten daylight hours with a mobilised network can disperse a big dairy herd**: the Bouwmans moved 450 head to seven farms by 6 PM on the day they heard of the breach and lost none when their farm flooded two days later ([Western Producer](https://www.producer.com/news/were-going-to-come-back-from-it/)). The same window saves only part of a poultry operation; broiler farmer Dave Martens moved about half his birds before "time ran out" and lost about 40,000 ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6256171)). **Twelve to 24 hours**, often overnight as in 2025, buys young stock, dry cows, selected poultry and hogs, equipment and feed. **Twenty-four to 48 hours** allows staged moves, booked haulers and receiving farms, and early shipment to processors. **Two weeks**, the manure-pit lead, is beyond any forecast. The binding constraints are receiving farms able to milk, trailers and drivers (mostly neighbours'), roads, darkness and labour. One dairy farmer fielded "up to 100 calls an hour" in 2021 ([BC Dairy](https://bcdairy.ca/press-releases/bc-dairy-update-cattle-evacuation-milk-supply/); [CBC/CP](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6280862)). A plaintiff in the 2021 class action put the species gap bluntly: with notice, dairy farmers "sure as heck could have got all their cattle out", but farms with 40,000 chickens would have had a harder time ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8504505/class-action-bc-floods-plaintiff-speaks/amp)).

Acting early has a price, and farmers pay it themselves until an alert is issued. Provincial support for livestock relocation starts at the evacuation-alert stage and runs through the local operations centre, which must agree there is significant risk. Costs are reimbursed to the local authority at market rates, and transport by a farm's own staff or family is not eligible ([EMCR Policy 2.01](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/public-safety-and-emergency-services/emergency-preparedness-response-recovery/embc/policies/201_provincial_support_for_livestock_relocation_policy.pdf); [BC Dairy](https://bcdairy.ca/dairy-farmers/livestock-relocation-and-reimbursement-program/)). A move triggered by a private early signal is therefore self-funded. Moves are costly even when justified: after their evacuation the Bouwmans saw milk production "well down" and breeding and calving cycles disrupted ([Western Producer](https://www.producer.com/news/were-going-to-come-back-from-it/)). Producers describe "the sense of being constantly under threat" and "this overhang of a flood coming" ([Country Life in BC](https://www.countrylifeinbc.com/producers-urged-to-make-emergency-plans/); [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7098766)). No source documents how many false alarms farmers will tolerate, so any threshold FloodLead sets is a hypothesis until farmers test it.

After 2025, farmers are not asking for better warnings. At the January 2026 roundtable convened by BC Dairy and BC Poultry, producers talked about stress, hard-to-access relief, the lack of "substantial, tangible flood mitigation action", and reluctance to expand or even stay; the City's summary does not mention warnings ([Abbotsford council blog](https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/considering-flood-impacts-we-continue-advocate-funding)). BC Dairy's priority is a new Sumas pump station ([BC Dairy](https://bcdairy.ca/press-releases/statement-from-bc-dairy-on-flood-conditions-in-the-fraser-valley/)). A new warning tool therefore has to beat the improved 2025 baseline by being earlier, more confident or more farm-specific. Simply existing is not enough.

## FloodLead's edge is timing, translation and proof, not raw skill

FloodLead BC's possible value splits into three layers that should never be blurred. The first is relay and translation: pushing warnings that already exist to a farmer's phone, in terms of that farm. The second is automated observation: firing when a gauge such as the SR 544 overflow gauge crosses a level, without waiting for a human decision. The third is genuine forecast skill: calibrated probabilities and arrival times that no one else produces. Only the third needs a model, and it is the hardest to prove.

Replays of both floods show what the first two layers alone would have bought. These are counterfactual estimates built from archived timestamps. In 2021, relaying the 3:40 PM Nov 14 NWS overflow warning would have reached opted-in farmers **about 9 hours before Abbotsford's first alert**, roughly a day before the first Sumas Prairie order and about 36 hours before water reached the Meier farm. A trigger on the SR 544 gauge crossing its 4 ft flood stage would have fired between about 6:30 and 8 PM that Sunday ([IEM 2021](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FLWSEW&sdate=2021-11-10&edate=2021-11-20&fmt=text&limit=400); [USGS 2021](https://nwis.waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/iv/?format=rdb&sites=12211200,12211190,12211195,12215100,12214550,12215000,12214500&parameterCd=00065,00060&startDT=2021-11-13T00:00-08:00&endDT=2021-11-19T00:00-08:00); [Vancouver Sun](https://vancouversun.com/news/local-news/significant-atmospheric-river-causing-rainfall-warnings-across-southern-b-c)). In 2025, relaying the 10 AM RFC Flood Warning and the 10:26 AM NWS overflow warning with a farm-specific translation would have arrived **about 5–7 daylight hours before the City's alert and about 12–13 hours before the order**. A lower "heads-up" tier relaying the RFC's Dec 9 Flood Watch would have come a day earlier still, and the Emerson Road gauge rise at 2:30 PM would have added a confirming trigger 1.5–3 hours ahead of the alert ([IEM 2025](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FLWSEW&sdate=2025-11-28&edate=2025-12-22&fmt=text&limit=600); [USGS 2025](https://nwis.waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/iv/?format=rdb&sites=12211200,12211190,12211195,12215100,12214550,12215000,12214500&parameterCd=00065,00060&startDT=2025-12-08T00:00-08:00&endDT=2025-12-14T00:00-08:00); [EmergencyInfoBC](https://www.emergencyinfobc.gov.bc.ca/event/flood-alert-abbotsford-10dec25/)). This is uncomfortable for a forecasting product: the largest and most certain gains come from automation, and they are largest when institutions lag, as in 2021. Whether a farmer would act on a text at 4 PM on a Sunday, with no floodwater yet in sight, is a separate question; the Bouwmans' "green grass" hesitation suggests many would not without local confirmation.

Where FloodLead can beat the status quo on measurable terms is narrower than "better forecasts", but it is real. The first edge is **resolution and cadence**. NWS publishes forecast values in 6-hour steps and in December 2025 promised statement updates about 8 hours apart, while COFFEE forecasts in daily steps, refreshes its observations once a day and runs only during rain events ([NWPS forecast](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/NRKW1/stageflow); [IEM 2025](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FLWSEW&sdate=2025-11-28&edate=2025-12-22&fmt=text&limit=600); [COFFEE page](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/fallfloods/map_coffee.html)). Hourly updates are trivially more frequent; the measurable question is whether they time threshold crossings at North Cedarville and the overflow gauges better than persistence, trend and the 6-hourly NWS forecast. The second is **calibration**. No official product says "60% chance of major flooding", and because the documented error is systematic (low above flood stage, worse with lead time), a probability that corrects it is a testable improvement on a deterministic "moderate" call, scored with Brier and reliability statistics. The third is **arrival time**: an hourly, gauge-driven estimate of when overflow water reaches the border and named roads, updated from the Everson gauges, competes against a 7-hour rule that 2025 broke. The fourth is **personal action levels**, staged thresholds that match how farmers actually behave: young stock and equipment at a low probability, haulers and receiving farms at a higher one, milking herds and poultry barns only at high confidence. The fifth is **delivery**: one opt-in SMS that works across Abbotsford, the FVRD and Chilliwack, at night, fills a gap the City's email and door-knocks leave open. The sixth is **proof**: a public, append-only, automatically scored ledger would be the first published verification record of any flood forecast in this chain.

On the Canadian tributaries the official comparator is weaker still. The RFC relied on US numbers to describe the overflow in December 2025, which suggests its Sumas-at-Huntingdon forecast does not simulate Nooksack overflow. This is an inference; the RFC does not say so ([RFC Flood Warning](https://bcrfc.env.gov.bc.ca/warnings/advisories/FWN_2025_12_10_1000_SC_upg.pdf)). An hourly, overflow-aware forecast for 08MH029 and an hourly probabilistic forecast for the Chilliwack at Vedder should beat daily deterministic COFFEE output at sub-daily leads. The fair test also compares them at matched daily resolution, where the RFC is not handicapped.

Where FloodLead cannot win, it should not try. **Beyond about a day, rainfall forecasts set the limit**: observation-only machine-learning models lose skill sharply after 24 hours, rainfall forecasts misplace storms most badly over small basins, and even NWS's skill over persistence fades by day 3 ([Liu et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-26-265-2022); [IIHR on BAMS 2025](https://iihr.uiowa.edu/all-news/2025/10/rainfall-forecast-good-or-bad-flood-forecasting-answer-scale-dependent); [Welles 2005](https://www.weather.gov/media/owp/oh/rfcdev/docs/EdwinWellesV6.pdf)). Matching NWRFC at 6–24 hours is plausible; beating it at 24–48 hours without better rainfall forecasts is not. Multi-day awareness already exists, since NWS's watch came five days out and atmospheric-river forecasters flagged a Category 5 storm about six days before the Dec 11 crests ([Wikipedia, 2025 floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Pacific_Northwest_floods)). The Fraser freshet is covered: the RFC's routing model forecasts flow at Hope within about ±200 m³/s at 12–24 hours and gives a 7–8-day heads-up in heat scenarios ([Lower Fraser forecasting, 2022](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/air-land-water/water/river-forecast/flow_and_water_level_forecasting_for_the_lower_fraser_river_2022.pdf)). Inundation depth and extent need a hydraulic model. The province's is not public, and the hydraulic model in the $3.3 million Sumas River watershed planning initiative announced in January 2026 is still "at various stages of completion" ([Abbotsford/Province release](https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/supporting-flood-mitigation-planning-sumas-prairie)). No gauge model forecasts a dike breach like Vye Road's, a pump failure at Barrowtown, flood boxes held shut by high Fraser or Vedder levels, or the moment a highway closes ([Abbotsford flood response](https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse)). FloodLead has no legal role: it cannot issue alerts or orders, and it cannot start relocation reimbursement. As an opt-in service it cannot reach farmers who never subscribe, the same coverage problem Alertable already shows, and it starts with no trust in a community where an unofficial message has already caused panic.

The overflow is also the worst possible machine-learning target. Large overflows into Canada are rare; officials cite 1990, 2020, 2021 and 2025 as the analogues. The dedicated Emerson Road record starts in January 2024 and is now offline, and USGS border gauges returned no data for either event. The channel keeps changing too, through sediment, levee damage, and WSDOT's planned 2026 replacement of the SR 544 culvert with a bridge "to let more floodwater through" ([USGS series catalog](https://waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/site/?format=rdb&sites=12211195,12211190,12211200,12210700,12214500&seriesCatalogOutput=true&outputDataTypeCd=iv); [WA Ecology TFI](https://ecology.wa.gov/ecologys-work-near-you/river-basins-groundwater/nooksack-transboundary-flood-initiative)). The literature is blunt. Models trained on a single basin underperform models trained on many basins. Google's published operational stage-forecasting system did not yet handle basins under 1,000 km² or flash floods. Even hourly LSTMs run with observed rainfall miss peak timing by about 3.5 hours and underestimate the highest 2% of flows by roughly 14–16% ([Kratzert et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-4187-2024); [Nevo et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02780); [Gauch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07921)). Overflow should be modelled as a threshold or hydraulic relation on Everson flows, with explicit, wide uncertainty, and re-fitted after the bridge, not learned from two events. Data latency adds friction: Canadian real-time data are normally posted within about two hours, with a service aim of six ([Wateroffice FAQ](https://wateroffice.ec.gc.ca/contactus/faq_e.html)).

## The bar for "thank God": a trusted daylight call a day ahead

Put the farmer evidence and the 2025 timeline together and the bar is clear. The zero-loss stories needed one to two days; the 2025 alert gave 12–18 hours, much of it overnight. A farmer says "thank God" when a message they trust arrives in daylight, at least a day before water reaches their road, early enough to book a trailer and a receiving farm, and then tells them through the night how many hours they have left. False alarms must also be rare enough that the cheap moves feel like insurance rather than harassment. The targets below follow from that evidence but have not been validated with farmers; they should be tested with BC Dairy and BC Poultry before they are fixed.

| Capability | Today's best comparator and how it did | FloodLead's realistic edge | Proposed bar to claim it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–6-day awareness | NWS Flood Watch ~5 days ahead; atmospheric-river outlooks ~6 days | None beyond relay | Make no claim |
| Daylight "prepare" call | City alert 12–18 h before water crossed (2025); ~9 h after the US overflow warning, with orders after the water (2021) | Relay, calibrated probability, farm translation | Fires ≥24 h before water reaches the border and ≥6 h before the City alert in most overflow events; false-alarm ratio ≤0.5 at this low-cost tier |
| "Move the herd" call | City order ~11 PM, ≤12 h before crossing (2025) | Arrival estimate driven by overflow gauges | ≥12 h before water reaches the farm's zone; POD ≥0.8 and FAR ≤0.2, matching NWS day-1 performance in published verification |
| North Cedarville crest, 12–48 h | NWS deterministic, ~2 ft low at ~1 day in both floods; "major" only after overflow began | Calibrated P(≥148 ft) and P(≥150 ft) that correct the low bias | No systematic low bias; reliable probabilities; Brier skill above zero against NWS-derived probabilities across all archived warnings, not just 2021 and 2025 |
| Overflow onset timing, 1–12 h | NWS areal warning ~3–4 h before onset; 6-h forecast steps | Hourly threshold-crossing forecasts from upstream gauges and observed rain | Positive skill against persistence and trend at every lead; smaller onset-timing error than NWS's issued forecasts |
| Water at the border and named roads | 7-hour rule; "early hours" projection; actual ~10–20 h | Hourly arrival estimate with ranges | Median arrival error ≤3 h; 80% ranges that contain the observed time about 80% of the time |
| Sumas at Huntingdon, Chilliwack at Vedder | COFFEE daily max/avg/min, deterministic, updated in rain events | Hourly, probabilistic, overflow-aware | Beat COFFEE at matched daily resolution and persistence at sub-daily leads |
| Fraser at Hope and Mission (freshet) | RFC routing, ±200 m³/s at 12–24 h | None | Relay the RFC; make no claim |
| Night delivery across jurisdictions | Email, web, social, door-knock (Abbotsford); opt-in Alertable (FVRD, Chilliwack); Alert Ready unused | One opt-in SMS with personal levels | Logged minutes from trigger to phone; low tiers held for daylight, high tiers sent immediately |
| Track record | None published for NWRFC Nooksack or BC RFC products | Append-only public ledger | Every forecast logged before the event; misses published |
| Depth, dike breaches, pumps, road closures, legal orders | Provincial hydraulics (non-public), City operations centre, ministry | None | Point to officials |

POD (probability of detection) is the share of real events the tool caught; FAR (false-alarm ratio) is the share of its alarms that ended with no event. The day-1 reference values come from [Welles 2005](https://www.weather.gov/media/owp/oh/rfcdev/docs/EdwinWellesV6.pdf); the comparator values come from the sources cited in the sections above.

The measurement design matters as much as the model, and it is where a team most easily fools itself. Every hindcast must **use only what was known at issue time**: archived numerical weather forecasts rather than the rain that actually fell, and provisional rather than revised gauge data. Any run fed observed future rain is a "perfect-forecast" upper bound and must be labelled so ([Gauch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07921); [Nevo et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02780)). **Each lead time is scored separately against persistence and trend**, the floor any AI model must clear ([Krajewski et al. 2021](https://doaj.org/article/4c2cfbdae0dd4b09a187c30eb60680c1); [NWS verification team](https://www.weather.gov/media/owp/oh/rfcdev/docs/NWS-Hydrologic-Forecast-Verification-Team_Final-report_Sep09.pdf)). Headline accuracy scores mean little at short leads: Google's operational models scored 0.987 on the standard Nash–Sutcliffe measure but only 0.67 measured against persistence ([Nevo et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02780)). **Events, not hours, are the unit**: a hit is a forecast threshold crossing matched to an observed one within a stated tolerance, because per-time-step scoring inflates results; one recent gradient-boosting paper's detection rate of 0.87 against 0.41 for Europe's EFAS rests on that kind of counting ([Bertoli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09336)). Lead time should be reported at a fixed false-alarm rate, with confidence intervals from resampling whole events or water years. November 2021 and December 2025 stay untouched as stress tests, never as tuning data. Ablations (gauges only, plus observed rain, plus forecast rain) keep relay and observation value from being passed off as model skill. And comparisons with the City's alert time must acknowledge that alerts are legal decisions with costs, not forecasts.

Honest proof will be slow. A perfect record of 5 hits in 5 events still leaves a 95% confidence interval for the hit rate that reaches down to about 0.48; 10 for 10 reaches down to about 0.69 (exact binomial interval, computed for this report). Officials cite only a handful of overflow analogues (1990, 2020, 2021 and now 2025), and the 2026 bridge resets the relationship that matters most. The public ledger should therefore be built on a ladder of thresholds. Frequent ones, such as action and minor stage at North Cedarville and high flows on the Chilliwack at Vedder, accumulate events every season. Rarer moderate, major and overflow thresholds come next, and claims there stay provisional and are labelled that way. The cheapest high-value first deliverable is retrospective: a scorecard of every archived NWS North Cedarville warning's crest forecast against the observed crest, built from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet text archive that preserves these products ([IEM archive](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=FLWSEW&sdate=2025-11-28&edate=2025-12-22&fmt=text&limit=600)). That alone would tell farmers how far to trust a "moderate" forecast, something no agency publishes.

Delivery strategy matters as much as accuracy. Reimbursement starts at the alert, and farmers trust their associations and the City's operations centre. FloodLead's earlier signals therefore do the most good when they also reach BC Dairy's and BC Poultry's representatives in that centre and the City's own decision-makers, where they can help bring the alert forward. The premises ID registry, mandatory since 2022 and already used to notify farms of nearby disease, is the obvious delivery list if the province ever opens it to flood warnings ([BC premises ID release](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2022AF0038-000871)). Every message should say plainly that it is a forecast, not an order, and link to the City's official map, so that FloodLead never repeats 2025's recirculated-order panic.

## Conclusion

The improvement between 2021 and 2025 came from institutions translating existing US forecasts faster, not from better forecasts. What remains broken sits on the Canadian side of the border, in the hours between the Everson overflow and the barn: how much water, when, and where. Nobody forecasts that, the overflow gauges that could anchor such a forecast are few and young, and the 2026 bridge will reshape the flow path. That gap, not the river forecast, is FloodLead's niche. The honest description of the product is a public, verified translation layer from Everson to the barn, not a better hydrological model than NWS.

Most of FloodLead's near-term value needs little machine learning. Relaying warnings to phones in farm terms and firing on gauge readings would have beaten the City by hours in both floods. The part that needs a model, calibrated one-to-two-day probabilities and arrival times, is exactly the part that will take years to prove and where the team is most likely to fool itself. FloodLead will matter most in the next 2021-style failure, when institutions lag; in a 2025-type event its job is to move a night-time scramble into daylight. Its most credible early contribution may be the scorecard itself. By publishing how official forecasts actually performed, it gives farmers a reason to trust, or discount, every warning they receive, including FloodLead's own.
